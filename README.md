# BC + Alberta Relocation Atlas

Interactive map of every BC and Alberta town of 10,000+ people (plus a faded 7,500 to 10,000 "maybe" layer), scored against Ryan's relocation criteria with adjustable weights.

**Live map:** https://westcoastrj.github.io/bc-ab-relocation-atlas/

## How it works
1. **Budget checks** (pass / fail / unknown / unverified): average rent within your budget (an estimated rent is "unverified" unless even its middle range is over budget), airport access only for towns more than ~8 h from Vancouver, and land, which is always **unverified** because the map shows assessed values of rural property nearby, not parcels actually for sale.
2. **Preference score:** each factor is a percentile among all 106 towns (a fixed comparison group). A missing value counts as the 25th percentile and is flagged in the rankings. A town with no rural area within an hour by road (Prince Rupert) scores as the worst for the rural factors; one where at least 10% of a nearby rural area's homes are only a little over the hour from its centre (61-75 min: Delta and Richmond) is left as missing instead, because other parts of a big municipality may be within the hour. An estimated rent is ranked at the top of its middle range. Default weights: RJC opportunity 25%, backup work 15%, rent 15%, rural property 15%, forest 10%, resilience/AI 10%, social 10%.

The AI switch (gradual vs fast takeoff) is a hand-set stress test on broad occupation groups, not a forecast. Rankings flag towns whose rank moves 5+ places between the two.

Wildfire/flood risk, schools, hospitals and winter climate are deliberately not scored.

## Known limits (no free data)
Land parcels for sale, current asking rents/listings, current job vacancies, commercial premises and property managers, sole-operator contractors, municipal renovation permits, operating costs (heating, fuel, insurance).

- **Rural property** comes from Statistics Canada's Canadian Housing Statistics Program (table 46-10-0093, 2024 assessment rolls from BC Assessment and Alberta municipalities): the median assessed value of single-detached houses and of vacant land for each rural area (regional-district electoral areas, Alberta counties and special areas). "Within an hour" is measured to where people live: every 2021 census dissemination area with homes (dwellings occupied by usual residents, so ski-resort condos and cottages don't count), timed by road from the town centre (OSRM, x0.81 calibration). In BC each one is located at its 5 blocks with the most such homes (2021 Geographic Attribute File, BC Data Catalogue copy; blocks over 100 km² are skipped when the area has smaller ones, because their point can sit far from the homes), each standing for its share of the homes (points on small islands with no bridge, such as Gambier, Keats and West Bamfield, don't count: their few roads aren't connected to the road network, so the router would time them from a road across the water; spotted where a point's own road is much closer than the road it was timed from and the line between them crosses water); in Alberta, where block counts can't be downloaded by a script (StatCan's www12 site blocks them), at its 3 smallest blocks, its most built-up spots. In BC, places whose fastest route uses a ferry don't count: each area's 3 biggest reachable neighbourhoods are route-checked, and if any needs a ferry, every reachable point in that area is; so is any point much slower than the rest of its neighbourhood (Thetis Island shares one with Saltair). Alberta's few small river ferries aren't checked. An area counts when at least 10% of its homes are within the hour. The town's figure is the median of all those properties pooled, each area counting with its number of properties x that share (each area's values modelled as lognormal around its median, with the typical spread implied by StatCan's medians and averages), so it doesn't jump between two very different areas the way a median of medians does. StatCan's median covers the whole area, so for an area only partly within the hour the house median is scaled by how 2021 census home values (census profile API, by neighbourhood) in that part compare with the whole area (marked "adj." on the card); where there are too few census values for that part (under 2 neighbourhoods or under half its homes) the whole-area figure is used, marked "whole"; if under half of such an area is within the hour, it's listed but not used for the town's figures (marked "not used": for example Kootenay Boundary Area E, which counts for Kelowna only through Big White). A town figure resting on under 100 properties' worth of homes (White Rock) is flagged as rough. Four Alberta counties have no StatCan figure (Kneehill, Westlock, Athabasca, Spirit River): their house value is estimated from those census values times the Alberta rural assessed/census ratio (marked "est."). Areas with under 50 properties are left out and listed. Assessed values usually trail sale prices. Fort McMurray's rural hamlets sit inside Wood Buffalo, which has no rural-only figure, so its value stays blank; Strathcona County's acreages (Ardrossan, Josephburg, Cooking Lake) aren't included for the same reason, and Edmonton-area cards say so. Lloydminster covers the Alberta side only.
- **Rent:** CMHC October survey averages. Eight small Alberta towns use the Government of Alberta's own Apartment Vacancy and Rental Cost Survey (May-Aug 2025) for rent and vacancy: the six CMHC skips (Drumheller, Edson, Hinton, Olds, Taber, Whitecourt) plus Innisfail and Banff, whose own survey replaces a neighbouring city's CMHC figure (the card shows both). CMHC has published no rent for four BC towns since at least 2019 (Sechelt surveyed but suppressed every year, Whistler suppressed or not listed, Revelstoke never listed, Kitimat last published in 2011), so their rent is an **estimate**: the 2021 census median renter cost times the median CMHC/census ratio in BC towns that have their own CMHC centre (census metropolitan areas and shared centres left out). The card shows the middle half and the full spread of that ratio, marks the value as an estimate and shows local cross-checks, which run higher. In the score an estimate is ranked at the top of its middle range. Their vacancy stays blank.

## How it updates
`.github/workflows/refresh-and-deploy.yml` runs at 13:17 and 01:17 UTC. `pipeline/build.py` checks CMHC (rent, reliability grade, vacancy) and StatCan business counts, validates coverage before using them, keeps each town's previous values if a source fails, and writes `site/data/status.json` (last checked, last successful fetch, reference period). `places.json` is only committed when the data actually changes. A failed source makes the run red, so GitHub emails the repo owner; the map also shows a warning banner if a refresh failed or none has run in 18 h.

Static inputs, rebuilt rarely:
- `pipeline/census_extract.py` → `pipeline/cache/census.json` (2021 Census tables from Statistics Canada)
- `pipeline/geo_extract.py` → `pipeline/cache/geo.json` (locations, drive times, airports, land cover)
- `pipeline/extra_extract.py` → `pipeline/cache/extra.json` (rural property assessments within an hour's drive, Alberta's rural rent survey, BC census renter costs; needs `unzip` and `grep`, and about an hour for the drive times)
